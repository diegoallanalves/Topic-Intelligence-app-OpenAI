**CONTEXT**

You are classifying U.S. Congressional actions for a
study of DAQO Group — a Chinese manufacturer of transformers and
electrical equipment (HTS heading 8504) — deciding between three
strategies: exporting from China, manufacturing in Mexico, or
manufacturing in the United States.

You are assigning ONE topic per Congressional
action. You are not judging
relevance, risk, opportunity, or policy stage. Topic only.

---

**YOUR INPUT**

For each row you are given three fields. **Read them
in this order.**

1. **Mechanism** —      one or two sentences, written by an earlier pass, naming the instrument.      This is your **first** source. It is short (about 22 words),      and only a quarter of these lines mention DAQO, so it is the least      polluted view of what the bill does.
2. **Analytical      Summary** — a longer paragraph (about 79 words) from the same pass.      Use it to **fill a gap** the Mechanism leaves, or to confirm.      99% of these mention DAQO and about a third of each one is about DAQO's      exposure rather than about the bill.
3. **Title** —      the bill's official name. It has three specific jobs, listed below.

Between them, Mechanism and Summary state a provision
in 95% of rows — more than either alone. Take the provision
from whichever states it most plainly, and prefer the Mechanism when both
do.

**What the Title is for — and what it is not for**

The Title is the only one of the three fields that was not
written by a machine, so it never invents anything. But it is also
the least informative: 130 of these titles are acronyms with no
content (CTF Act, APP Act, LEED Act, E-FRONTIER
Act), and Congress names bills for effect, not accuracy.

**Use the Title for exactly three things:**

1. **Tie-break      1.** The words "Appropriations Act", "continuing      resolution" and so on appear in the Title, never in the Mechanism. 77      rows are decided this way.
2. **Tie-break      2a, the named-place topics.** If the Title names **Taiwan**, **Hong      Kong**, or **Uyghur / Xinjiang**, that is decisive — see the      rule below.
3. **Fallback.** When      neither the Mechanism nor the Summary states a provision (55 rows), build      the PROVISION line from the Title and score 70.

**Never use the Title for anything else.** If a
provision exists, it beats the Title every time. "Countering
Chinese Propaganda Act" that imposes sanctions is
Sanctions & Export Controls, not
Political / Foreign Influence. A title is a claim about what a
bill is called; the provision is a fact about what it does.

**But understand what these two fields are.** Neither
was written to describe the bill. It was written to answer a different
question: *is this relevant to DAQO, and is it a risk or an opportunity?* So
it contains two kinds of sentence mixed together:

**THE TWO-STEP METHOD — do not skip step 1**

**STEP 1 — Extract the provision**

Before you choose a topic, write the **PROVISION** line: one
sentence saying what the bill
obliges, prohibits, taxes, funds, authorises or
conditions, and on whom.

**The PROVISION line must obey these constraints:**

- It      must NOT contain the word **DAQO**.
- It      must NOT contain **could**, **may**, **might**, **potentially**, **likely**, **reflects**, **signals**, **sentiment**,      or **risk**.
- It      must name a **verb of legal effect**: prohibits, bars, requires,      imposes, levies, authorises, appropriates, funds, establishes, directs,      restricts, suspends, withdraws, designates, amends, conditions, screens,      certifies, repeals, waives, allocates.
- It      must be drawn from the Mechanism, the Analytical Summary, or failing both,      the Title. Do not invent a provision.

If neither the Mechanism nor the Summary states a
provision — only that the bill is relevant, or only what it
signals — write exactly:

PROVISION: NONE STATED

and classify from the Title alone. Never manufacture a
provision to fill the line.

**STEP 2 — Classify the PROVISION line**

Choose the topic that matches **the sentence you just
wrote**. Do not re-read the Mechanism or the Summary at this
stage. Do not classify on a word that appears only in the part of the
summary you excluded.

This is the whole point of the method: a word that did
not survive into your PROVISION line cannot drive your topic.

**THE TRANSFORMER TRAP**

The earlier pass was told DAQO makes transformers, and
it habitually ends sentences with a consequence clause naming the product:

"...which could lead to continued trade restrictions on
Chinese goods, **including transformers**." "...poses a risk
to DAQO's ability to export **transformers** to the U.S."

The word **transformer** appears in 265 of
these summaries — in bills about market-economy
status, propaganda, the Caribbean, Saudi WMD, and pension
funds. **It is the earlier pass naming DAQO's product. It is almost never
a statement that the bill regulates transformers.**

Because "transformer" appears in
DAQO-consequence sentences, it will not survive into a correctly written
PROVISION line. **If "transformer" is in your PROVISION line,
check it again.** It belongs there only when the bill's own provision
acts on transformers — for example, H.R. 6938, which
funds "the domestic supply chain for the manufacture of distribution
and power transformers."

The same trap applies to **market access**, **supply
chain**, **grid**, **electrical equipment** and **tariffs** where
those words appear only in a consequence clause. In the previous run these
were the four largest sources of error.

**TIE-BREAKS — a strict ladder, first match wins**

Work down the list. The **first** rule
that matches is the answer; stop there. Do not weigh a lower rule
against a higher one.

1. **The      Title contains "Appropriations Act", "continuing      resolution", "continuing appropriations", "making      appropriations" or "supplemental appropriations"** → **Appropriations      Vehicles**. Regardless of the clause inside, and regardless of which      department it funds. *A "Department of State, Foreign      Operations, and Related Programs Appropriations Act" is an      Appropriations Vehicle, not State Department & Foreign Aid.*

2a. **The Title names Taiwan, Hong Kong, or Uyghur /
Xinjiang** → use **Taiwan**, **Hong Kong**, or **Forced
Labor & Xinjiang** respectively. These three topics exist to be
counted as places, so a Taiwan bill must land in Taiwan even when its
instrument is a tariff or an aid provision — otherwise the Taiwan
count becomes arbitrary. **One exception, and it matters:** where
the Title names Uyghur or Xinjiang, choose between the two candidate
topics by the instrument. An import ban, entity list, rebuttable
presumption or supply-chain disclosure → **Forced Labor &
Xinjiang**. A genocide determination, sanctions on
officials, or a statement of condemnation with no trade
instrument → **Human Rights & Political Repression**. This
split decides whether the row counts as direct or indirect exposure, so do
not guess: if no instrument is stated, use Human
Rights & Political Repression.

2b. **The PROVISION acts on an energy or electrical
thing** — grid, bulk-power
system, transmission, substation, transformer, switchgear, generation, solar, photovoltaic, nuclear, oil, gas, the
Strategic Petroleum Reserve, critical minerals → **Energy, Grid
& Electrical Infrastructure**. **This beats rule 3.** "Keep
China Out of Solar Energy Act" prohibits federal procurement of solar
panels; solar is an energy product, so it is **Energy**, not
Federal Procurement.

3. **The      PROVISION restricts what the government or its contractors may buy** → **Federal      Procurement & Buy American**. Applies where the product is not an      energy product — school milk, uniforms, drones, pharmaceuticals.
4. **The      PROVISION restricts who may own or acquire a US asset** → **Foreign      Investment in the US**. **The PROVISION restricts money, its      price, or access to capital** → **Currency, Financial &      Funding**.
5. **The      PROVISION screens or restricts visas** →
   - PRC       nationals, Chinese researchers, students, military-affiliated applicants       → **Information & Technology Security**
   - migration       across the southern border, asylum, the wall, border patrol → **Border       Security & Migration**
6. **The      bill's subject is a third country and China is only context** —      Russia, Ukraine, Iran, the Baltics, Korea, the Arctic, the Philippines,      Africa, the Caribbean, Venezuela → **Defense, Military &      Territorial Disputes**. Unless the PROVISION is itself a sanction,      tariff or export control on Chinese entities, in which case use that      topic. *"Stop China's Financing of Russia's Invasion Act"      imposes trade penalties on Chinese firms → Tariffs, Trade Remedies &      Market Access.*
7. **Political      / Foreign Influence** — last resort, never first.
